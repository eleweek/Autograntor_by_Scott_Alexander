# Proposed rulings — run 2026-10-21_fva

### CL-84: Career-entry ΔP for short paid research mentorships [Proposed 2026-10-21, by Claude]
**Rule:** When a grant pays a junior person for a short research mentorship, price their entry into safety work against the Proposed "junior researcher enters a sustained AI-safety research role" milestone, at a per-mentee ΔP anchored on a full-time, MATS-scale programme (roughly 0.3–0.35 career entries per scholar). Scale that anchor down for the mentorship's FTE-months relative to a MATS cohort, for the mentor's seniority and availability during the mentorship, and for whether a placement pipeline exists. State each factor. Carry the risk that the mentees are never recruited in the correlated execution factor, not in the ΔP as well.
**Why:** Without a shared anchor, estimators priced the same short mentorship at per-mentee shifts differing by 2× or more. Talent rows can be the largest source of disagreement in small research grants.
**Example (hypothetical):** A full-time three-month programme with a dedicated senior mentor and a placement pipeline might sit at about half the MATS-scale shift. A half-time two-month stipend with a part-time mentor and no pipeline might sit at about a twentieth.

### CL-85: Pilots that claim to de-risk larger programmes [Proposed 2026-10-21, by Claude]
**Rule:** When an applicant's main argument is that a pilot's write-up is cheap evidence for much larger bets on the same thesis, price that evidence row through the money channel. Value it at P(the write-up is read by the larger programmes' decision-makers) × a stated small fraction of their budgets redirected or improved × the CL-4 money price, plus ordinary readership value. Never price it by comparison with mass-audience social-shift entries. Name the larger programmes in the report.
**Why:** A niche technical retrospective is not a public moment. Comparing it with mass-media milestones overstated its value severalfold relative to the mechanism the applicant actually claims.
**Example (hypothetical):** A pilot report has a 50% chance of being read by a $100M programme and would improve 0.5% of that budget. At 20 per $1M it is worth about 0.5 × 0.5 × 20 = 5, plus readership.
