# Proposed rulings — run 2026-10-22_pauseai-local

### CL-89: Stipends paid to people who already volunteer [Proposed 2026-10-22, by Claude (judge)]
**Rule:** When a grant pays stipends to existing volunteer organisers at a mostly unpaid organisation, set the stipend line's share of the organisation-year as (the volunteer line's share of the organisation's output) × (the uplift in that line's output from paid hours), never as the stipend's share of the cash budget. Default the uplift to about 0.3–0.5 of the proportional increase in hours when recipients already volunteer and the obligations attached to the stipend are ones active volunteers already meet; state the fraction of paid hours that are additional. Apply the resulting share in full to rows the volunteer line produces (local events, local press, network capacity, organiser careers) and at about half to rows that run mainly through the organisation's central staff (national lobbying, treaty-level probability shifts). CL-54 still sets the average-dollar comparator.
**Why:** The two bases differ by about 2× for the same stipend line, and the cash-share base moves with an unknown budget while the output-share base does not. Without a stated split, central policy rows inherit a share that local volunteer activity does not drive.
**Example (hypothetical):** Local groups produce 40% of an organisation's output; stipends raise local hours by 80%, of which half were already volunteered, so output rises about 30%: share 0.12 on local rows, 0.06 on central rows, whatever the cash budget.

### CL-90: Values-branch cancellation, and offsets across two values questions [Proposed 2026-10-22, by Claude (judge)]
**Rule:** When a CL-11 "harm" answer means the grant's activity fails to activate an outcome rather than pushing it the other way, price the harm branch as cancellation (the row goes to zero), not reversal, unless a mechanism for reversal is stated. Never build an offset row from the sum of other rows' good-corner values: in corners where a second values question has already flipped a row's sign, such an offset subtracts value that is already negative. When a row's sign depends on one question and its magnitude on another, either split it into one row per answer pair or fold the second question's effect as a stated factor, and report the unfolded corners.
**Why:** A single offset row built from one corner's totals double-charges the mixed corners and overstates P(negative); cancellation and reversal differ by 2× on the same evidence. This refines CL-17 and complements CL-82, which covers reversal for attention-maximising tactics specifically.
