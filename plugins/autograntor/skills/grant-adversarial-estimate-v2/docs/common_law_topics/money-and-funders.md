# Common Law topic file — money, funders and the no-grant world

Verbatim copies of Proposed rulings from Part III of `claude/common_law.md` (frozen original, version 2026-10-20.1), grouped by topic. The original remains the source of truth. Rulings in this file: CL-14, 21, 23, 28, 34, 35, 41, 43, 44, 53, 63, 64, 65, 66, 72, 75, 83. Read it when the grant raises questions about pending or outside money, funging, runway, absorptive capacity, refunds or unrestricted money.

### CL-14: Pending applications at other funders [Proposed 2026-10-16, by Claude]
**Rule:** An application already pending with another funder is neither a pledge (CL-8 "existing funds and pledges") nor "a later round" (CL-8 "don't assume a later round rescues the project"). Model it as an event with a stated probability in both the grant and the no-grant worlds. The probability may differ between the two worlds only for a stated reason (the grant lowers the other funder's urgency, or raises the applicant's credibility), and the difference should be small. Never treat it as secured or as zero in one world only.
**Why:** Ignoring it overstates bridge grants; counting it as secured understates them; and treating it asymmetrically (secured with the grant, absent without) is the error that actually moves estimates, because a symmetric pending application lifts both worlds and leaves the grant's increment nearly unchanged.

### CL-21: Freed backstop [Proposed 2026-10-16, by Claude]
**Rule:** When a grant relieves a named party of a committed backstop, credit the expected dollars freed at the CL-4 money price × P(the backstop would have been drawn).
**Why:** This is the mirror image of CL-4. Money the grant releases elsewhere has value just as money it pulls in has cost.

### CL-23: Reported-but-unlisted grants and per-year survival rows [Proposed 2026-10-16, by Claude]
**Rule:** A grant another funder reportedly made, but which is missing from that funder's public list, is a pledge at a stated probability, kept the same in both worlds (as in CL-14). For an organization whose death without the grant is not imminent, use per-year "operating at scale" probabilities with partial-loss states (downsizing, not just closure). Let the no-grant path include ordinary future fundraising (CL-8), and move the probabilities only in the years the grant's banked runway actually covers.
**Why:** A single binary "survives" row overstates bridge grants to organizations that would likely limp on. Treating an unconfirmed grant as certain, or as zero, swings the no-grant world.

### CL-28: Earned revenue versus follow-on grants [Proposed 2026-10-17, by Claude]
**Rule:** Product revenue the grantee earns (sales, tickets) is earned revenue under CL-8, not a CL-4 charge. Follow-on grants the grantee says it will seek are charged at the CL-4 money price, with the probability raised (≈×1.8) if the product shows traction and lowered (≈×0.7) otherwise. Grant-funded continuation after 2030 is charged inside its own row, not again in the follow-on row.
**Why:** CL-4 charges resources pulled from elsewhere; sales are not; and charging continuation twice double-counts (CL-5).

### CL-34: Funging with an intended but unsubmitted application to a repeat funder [Proposed 2026-10-18, by Claude]
**Rule:** An application the applicant says it will make to a funder that has funded it before is modelled like a pending one (CL-14): same probability in both worlds, set from the funder's history and any forecast. Unlike CL-14, the *expected award* may fall with the grant for the stated reason that the applicant writes its own ask around the remaining gap and the other funder caps on runway; the reduction should be convex in grant size (small when the grant leaves the plan visibly unfilled, approaching the other funder's usual award once the grant covers the plan year) and capped at that award. Net the reduction from the grantee's resources and credit it at the CL-4 money price (CL-21 logic). Never let the probability differ between worlds without a stated mechanism.
**Why:** Treating the unsubmitted application as zero overstates the grant; treating it as secured understates it; and a flat funging fraction misses that the applicant's ask, not the funder's taste, is what the grant changes.

### CL-35: Grant within the ask but above absorptive capacity [Proposed 2026-10-18, by Claude]
**Rule:** CL-22 applies whenever the grant plus funding received or expected after the application was written exceeds what the applicant can spend in the plan year, even if the grant is within the ask. Cap the plan-year spend increment from the applicant's own hiring-rate statements (as a distribution, not a point), treat the rest as banked runway valued at the mixture of (a) the money price for the share that displaces the applicant's ordinary next-year fundraising (CL-8) and (b) the marginal program value for the share that becomes extra next-year program, add a signed idling/discipline charge above a stated threshold, and report the uncapped figure as a sensitivity.
**Why:** An ask written before other funders decided is not the applicant's need; without a cap the top tranche inherits the first tranche's value and fails CL-9.

### CL-41: Applicant-stated plan ambition raises the absorptive cap [Proposed 2026-10-18, by Claude]
**Rule:** When the applicant says in its own words that funding commitments set how aggressively it plans (rather than naming a fixed plan), raise the CL-35 absorptive cap by a stated fraction of the grant (≈0.3) instead of holding it fixed, and say so. Keep CL-35's hiring-rate cap on the FTE part of the increment.
**Why:** CL-35 assumes the plan year is fixed before the grant; an applicant that scales its contractor program to commitments can spend more of a large grant in-year, so a fixed cap banks money that would be spent.

### CL-43: Funders outside the platform keep their independent money in the no-grant world [Proposed 2026-10-18, by Claude; amended by the human user 2026-10-18]
**Rule:** The prompt's "if I rejected it, nobody else would approve it" and CL-8's "this money is not replaced" mean that the platform's money is not replaced. Independent allocations to the same organisation from funders outside the platform (donor-adviser recommendations, pledges, other funders' rounds and applications) stay in both worlds at whatever probability they would happen anyway (CL-14/CL-34 logic). Other evaluators' allocations on the same platform are not outside money: per Adopted CL-62 they are left out of both worlds.
**Why:** Estimators read the prompt both ways; removing outside funders' independent money from the no-grant world inflates the gap the grant fills and the marginal factor.

### CL-44: Adviser-recommended, unfilled funding and funding-state blending [Proposed 2026-10-18, by Claude]
**Rule:** A donor adviser's recommendation to its clients that has not been filled is pending money (CL-14/CL-34), not a pledge and not zero. State the probability of each funding state by a stated date (e.g. the next hiring tier still unfilled; filled; the adviser's larger recommendation substantially filled), identical in both worlds unless the grant is ≥ ~20% of the recommendation. Value the grant as the blend across states, with a marginal factor per state: the unfilled-tier slice at the CL-37 non-founder bound, session-critical lines of the next tier below that, and money on top of a filled plan at the plan's weaker lines (CL-22 logic). Money that arrives before the adviser's money can still buy session-critical lines, so the filled-state factor is not the plan's weakest line.
**Why:** Policy organisations often carry large unfilled recommendations; treating them as secured zeroes the grant, treating them as absent overstates it, and a two-state blend hides the intermediate case.

### CL-53: Applicant minimum-size statements with pending money [Proposed 2026-10-17, by Claude]
**Rule:** When an applicant says it will not run a project below a minimum, the no-grant world runs it if, and only if, independent pending money (at its CL-14 probability) reaches that minimum. In that state, the grant's increment is the top-up, valued with any named full-time threshold premium. Leftover money extends the activity at the post-window value, with CL-38 logic.

### CL-63: Option-value grants to a well-funded organisation with no stated plan [Proposed 2026-10-19, by Claude]
**Rule:** When an applicant with more than a year of runway applies "for option value" without naming what the money buys, value the grant as the CL-35 mixture (in-year cap; banked money split between displaced fundraising at the money price and next-year program at a CL-22-diminished rate) plus at most one *ambition* row: a stated shift in the probability that the organisation's next flagship output happens, sublinear across tiers and no more than about 0.02 per 40% of annual budget, on a node priced net of the flagship's effects already carried by the organisation's continuous rows (CL-5). Never credit the flagship's existence to the grant; add at most one small reach/launch row, larger only if the applicant shows launch spend was budget-limited.
**Why:** "Money in the bank changes what we dare to do" is a real effect, but without a bound it can be claimed for any well-funded organisation, and the flagship node is easily counted again on top of the continuous rows that already contain it.
**Example (optional):** An organisation with $5M of cash and a $4M budget, whose flagship outputs are worth 6,000 net of its continuous rows, receives $2M (half a budget-year): ambition row ≤ 0.025 × 6,000 = 150, on top of the CL-35 program and money rows.

### CL-64: Distraction row for money above absorptive capacity [Proposed 2026-10-19, by Claude]
**Rule:** For money above an organisation's absorptive capacity, where the applicant has named speculative lines that evaluators judge weaker than its core work, add a signed event row "leaders are pulled into weaker projects": probability rising steeply with the excess (roughly 5% at a fifth of a budget-year of excess, rising to roughly 25% at a full budget-year or more), costing roughly a tenth of a senior-team year on the organisation's own ledger. Keep it separate from CL-22's idling charge (money that sits) and CL-36 (bad hires), and do not multiply it by the execution factor.
**Why:** CL-22 and CL-36 cover idle money and headcount jumps, but not the case evaluators most often raise for well-funded small teams: the money gets used, on the wrong thing, by the people whose time is the organisation's value.

### CL-65: Debts owed to the platform operator or to an evaluator's organisation [Proposed 2026-10-19, by Claude]
**Rule:** Money an applicant owes to the platform operator or to an evaluator's organisation reduces effective runway identically in both worlds; repayment is never credited as value; and the creditor's evaluator opinions are discounted for the conflict under CL-12. Price the conflict only as a small funder-specific exposure row (CL-32 scale, a few percent of about −100), reported with and without per CL-57.
**Why:** A receivable can be misread as a reason to fund (the creditor is repaid) or not to fund (the applicant is poorer than it looks); it is neither, and the conflict belongs in the weighting of evidence, not the ledger.

### CL-66: Displacement share when the applicant's ordinary funder is a routine patron [Proposed 2026-10-19, by Claude]
**Rule:** When an applicant's ordinary annual fundraising comes mainly from a patron or fund that has demonstrably given without regard to the applicant's cash balance, set CL-35(a)'s displaced share from the applicant's likely change in its own ask (CL-34: small while its plan is undefined or visibly unfilled, convex in grant size, approaching the usual award once runway exceeds about two years), not from runway arithmetic. State the evidence for the patron's behaviour (e.g. a recent award made while the applicant held a large balance).
**Why:** Runway arithmetic assumes funders fill gaps; a patron that funds on enthusiasm does not, so more of a grant becomes extra program than the gap-filling model implies, and this share can swing a banked-money grant's value substantially.

### CL-72: Merged funding targets and applicant cash above the scored ask [Proposed 2026-10-20, by Claude]
**Rule:** When the scored application shares one funding target with a sibling application from the same organisation (so the platform cannot keep the money to the scored line), or the organisation's cash already covers the scored line's plan, value the grant as unrestricted money. Allocate it across lines by the organisation's own statements about which lines scale with extra money and which it would cut first without it, plus banked runway (CL-35). Report the value if the money were restricted to the scored line as a sensitivity, and tell the grantmaker when a restriction would change the answer.
**Why:** Valuing fungible money as if it bought the line named in the application overstates it whenever the organisation's marginal dollar goes to a different, weaker or more contested line.
**Example (optional):** An organisation holds $8M against a $3M plan for its research line and is asking separately for $7M for a communications line it calls its main effort; an extra $1M mostly expands communications or banks, so the research ledger prices only the research share.

### CL-75: Matching pledges at organisations whose ordinary fundraising dwarfs the pledge [Proposed 2026-10-20, by Claude]
**Rule:** When an outside funder's matching pledge is small relative to the organisation's ordinary annual fundraising, set P(the grant is pivotal to unlocking the match) at about 0.1 or less, since the matching donations will arrive anyway. Value any money the grant does unlock at the grant's own marginal rate net of the CL-4 money price, never at the organisation's average dollar.
**Why:** Crediting the full match, or valuing it at the average dollar, credits the grant with money other donors were going to supply.

### CL-83: Restriction sensitivity that falls below the unrestricted value [Proposed 2026-10-20, by Claude]
**Rule:** Under CL-72, when the restricted-to-scored-line value is lower than the unrestricted value (because the organisation's other lines or reserves beat the scored line's last dollar), say so and tell the grantmaker a restriction would reduce value; the platform's inability to restrict is then not a cost and needs no charge.
**Why:** CL-72 is written for the case where fungibility hurts; without the converse, estimators may charge for unrestrictability even when it helps.
