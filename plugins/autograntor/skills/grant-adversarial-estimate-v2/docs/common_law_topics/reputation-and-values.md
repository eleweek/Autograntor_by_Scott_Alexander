# Common Law topic file — reputation, conflicts and values questions

Verbatim copies of Proposed rulings from Part III of `claude/common_law.md` (frozen original, version 2026-10-20.1), grouped by topic. The original remains the source of truth. Rulings in this file: CL-17, 20, 27, 32, 42, 73, 74, 76, 79, 81, 82. Read it whenever a grant has a CL-11 values question, a hostile-press or scandal row, a leader's conflict of interest, a troubled parent organisation, or withheld plans.

### CL-17: Scope of a CL-11 values branch [Proposed 2026-10-16, by Claude]
**Rule:** When pricing a grant under each answer to a contested values question, change only the rows whose sign or size depends on that answer, plus rows whose empirical premise is strongly correlated with it (state the correlation). Values-neutral rows (observation, evals, infrastructure, talent pipeline, charges) are identical across branches. Give the mixture weight in the headline and both branch totals.
**Why:** Attaching a general pessimism multiplier to every row turns a values question into a confidence haircut and double-counts the downside rows.

### CL-20: Grants to a project of a troubled parent or fiscal sponsor [Proposed 2026-10-16, by Claude]
**Rule:** A project with no separate legal existence shares its parent's liabilities and reputation. Price these as signed rows, never as a general haircut (CL-17): (i) liability/fungibility exposure, as the expected fraction of program value lost to the parent's obligations; (ii) an empirical incident or scandal row; and (iii) the values-laden part, such as harm to community trust norms, under CL-11. All three are usually sublinear in grant size. Note that a restricted or pay-for-service structure removes most of (i) and (ii).
**Why:** These risks are real but specific. A blanket discount hides which part is a factual risk and which is a values call for the human user.

### CL-27: Reputational charge for deliberately edgy products [Proposed 2026-10-17, by Claude]
**Rule:** When a funded product is deliberately provocative (e.g. shock-value or deliberately offensive content), price a signed event "a hostile piece names the funder": probability ≈3% base, plus about 1 point per $1M above the ask, ×3 if the product gets press-worthy traction; cost for a one-news-cycle story ≈ 1/140 of the OpenAI-board-crisis entry (≈ −50). Put the funder's tolerance for this exposure in a CL-11 branch rather than a general haircut.
**Why:** The story is about the funder's judgment, so its odds rise with the cheque and with the product's visibility; without a rule, estimators' prices for the same event differed by orders of magnitude.

### CL-32: Already-realised reputational events and founder-profile risk [Proposed 2026-10-17, by Claude]
**Rule:** A hostile story that has already run before the decision is not charged to the grant, but it raises the probability of a further story (the subject is now on reporters' lists) and the realised story's content is evidence about who a sequel would name. CL-27 prices risk from a provocative product; reputational risk from founders' or leaders' personal public profiles (e.g. past personal posts, a co-founder's public brand, an organisational conduct culture) is a separate signed row: probability set from the existing press trail (≈8–12% per grant year for a story that reaches the funder), cost ≈1/70 of the OpenAI-board-crisis entry (≈ −100), doubled in the CL-11 "brand is a harm" branch, with a separate low-probability serious-incident row (≈3% per residential cohort, ≈ −250) for programs that house young participants.
**Why:** Charging a story that has already run double-counts; applying CL-27's 3% product base to personal edginess under-prices; and a general haircut hides which part is empirical and which is a values call.

### CL-42: Pricing a sequel story by the realised bite of the stories that already ran [Proposed 2026-10-18, by Claude]
**Rule:** When hostile coverage of the applicant has already run, use its observed effect on the outcomes the applicant works on (did a bill die, did an opposing push gain a talking point) to bound the cost of a further story: the sequel's cost is at most a few times the realised one, scaled up only for a named new audience (a hearing, a governor, a coalition partner). For an advocacy organisation in a live legislative fight the ceiling is about 1/12 of the OpenAI-board-crisis entry (≈ −600) per story; CL-32's ≈ −100 remains the price for a story that reaches only the funder.
**Why:** Without a bound, estimators priced the same event across a several-fold range; the realised stories are the only calibration available.

### CL-73: A leader's conflict of interest at an evaluation organisation [Proposed 2026-10-20, by Claude]
**Rule:** When a leader of an organisation that publishes evaluations of companies' models works for, or is paid by, one of those companies, price the conflict as separate signed rows, each channel once: (i) a credibility haircut on the evaluation rows only; (ii) a public-story row scaled by the grant's share of activity (CL-40), with a one-time uptake dent on evaluation rows in story worlds only; (iii) an actual-bias row with a non-zero no-grant probability; and (iv) a small positive row for adoption of the evaluations through the conflicted role, net of overlap and not counted as independent validation. Put harm to community trust norms from funding the leader in a CL-11 branch, not in these rows. Whether the role was publicly disclosed before the decision moves (i) and (ii), not the values branch.
**Why:** Folding a conflict into one haircut double-counts some channels and drops others; adoption by the conflicted company is weak evidence of quality, so it should not raise the evaluation rows.

### CL-74: Reporting grants whose sign turns on values questions [Proposed 2026-10-20, by Claude]
**Rule:** When CL-11 branches flip the sign of a grant, report, alongside the CL-16 spread with the branches sampled, the spread with branch weights fixed and every corner total, so the human user can re-weight. Say that P(negative) with sampled branches mostly measures the chance the user's values resolve against the grant. When two values questions rest on overlapping evidence, correlate them at a stated coefficient rather than treating them as independent or identical.
**Why:** Sampling values branches as if they were outcome risk mixes a decision input with a forecast, and either independence or full correlation misstates the corners.

### CL-76: Endorsement and trust-norm rows under CL-62 grow with the cheque [Proposed 2026-10-20, by Claude]
**Rule:** Because CL-62 scores the grant as the whole platform's decision, values-branch rows for the signal a grant sends (endorsing a contested leader, harm to trust norms) grow with grant size at about G^0.7, still sublinear as CL-20 requires. State the exponent and report the flat (size-independent) version as a sensitivity.
**Why:** A flat charge treats a token grant and a programme-scale grant as the same endorsement; a linear charge treats the signal as if it were spending.

### CL-79: Applications that withhold their plans [Proposed 2026-10-20, by Claude]
**Rule:** When an applicant keeps its project details confidential (available only on request or under NDA), apply the CL-30 no-audit default to its reach claims, widen the execution dispersion in the CL-16 simulation, and add a signed fungibility event row if the funder cannot verify how the money is used. Do not add a separate secrecy haircut. List the withheld details as open questions, and re-score if the funder obtains them.
**Why:** Withholding detail is evidence about uncertainty, not about sign; a haircut double-counts what the no-audit default and the wider spread already price, while ignoring it lets unverifiable claims pass at face value.

### CL-81: Unverified integrity allegations in evaluator notes, and late self-disclosure [Proposed 2026-10-20, by Claude]
**Rule:** When an evaluator alleges misconduct or an undisclosed conflict by an applicant's leader that cannot be verified from public sources, carry it at a stated probability inside the empirical exposure and consequence rows only (CL-73 structure where it is a conflict). Weight the probability per CL-12 (discount for a declared adversarial stance, credit for plausible private access), put any values-based harm in a CL-11 branch, and list verification as an open question. A conflict the leader self-discloses after evaluators raised it but before the decision counts as disclosed for CL-73 (i) and (ii), but the earlier non-disclosure remains evidence for the CL-11 trust weight and raises sequel-story probability under CL-32.
**Why:** Ignoring such an allegation discards real evidence, while treating it as fact lets one evaluator's view decide the sign; a stated probability keeps the effect visible and easy to update when the grantmaker asks one direct question.

### CL-82: Values objections to attention-maximising persuasion [Proposed 2026-10-20, by Claude]
**Rule:** A contested claim that a persuasion programme's attention-economy tactics (scripted clips, populist framing, paid amplification) are net-harmful on discourse or epistemic-commons grounds, independent of the empirical backfire and exposure rows already priced, is a CL-11 values question. In the harm branch only, reverse a stated fraction of the glance and long-form Output rows; leave Outcome, elite and owned-channel rows unchanged; state the branch weight and its correlation with any leader-integrity question (CL-74). Never apply it as a haircut on all rows.
**Why:** Evaluators can agree a campaign moves opinion and still disagree about whether moving it that way is good; folding that into empirical downside rows hides a values call that can flip the sign.
